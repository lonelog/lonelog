# Lorelog

*A Standard Notation for Worldbuilding Logs*

Sep 25, 2026 · @Roberto Bisceglie

## 1. Introduction

Lorelog is a lightweight shorthand for recording how a fictional world gets built: the questions you ask, the facts you decide, the tensions those facts create, and how each decision spreads through the rest of the setting.

Worldbuilding has the same problem solo play has. Decisions pile up fast, and a week later you cannot remember why the harbor is walled, who decided the Guild pays its wardens per arrest, or which fact you quietly changed last month. Prose notes bury the decisions. Bullet lists lose the reasons. Lorelog records the reasoning chain itself, so your setting notes stay lean and the log keeps the history.

### 1.1 Lorelog and Lonelog

Lorelog is a sibling of **Lonelog**, the notation for solo RPG session logging. The two share a family resemblance and one practical component: the **tag grammar** for persistent elements (`[N:Name|tags]`, `[L:Name|tags]` and so on). That shared grammar means a play log and a world log can sit in the same notebook or vault and stay searchable together.

The core symbols are different, because the activity is different. Lonelog records **uncertainty resolved through play**: an action, a roll, a consequence. Lorelog records **a world decided step by step**: a question or a need, a fact, the friction it creates, and its ripple through the setting.

### 1.2 What Lorelog Does

Lorelog helps you:

- **Record decisions with their reasons**, so you can revisit them without guesswork
- **Keep consequences visible**, including the tensions and problems a fact creates
- **Track the backlog** of open questions and story needs
- **Trace changes** when you revise or retract a fact
- **Share your process** with collaborators or readers who know the notation

The notation is designed to be:

- **Method-agnostic:** it works with any way of building a world: top-down or bottom-up, story-driven or curiosity-driven, alone or in a group, following a published method, playing a worldbuilding game, or prepping a campaign
- **Layered:** usable as bare shorthand or with rich notes
- **Searchable:** tags and prefixes make every element easy to find
- **Format-agnostic:** it works in Markdown files and in paper notebooks

### 1.3 How to Use This Notation

This is a **toolbox, not a rulebook**. At its core are five symbols:

- `?` for a question you want answered
- `!` for a need a story imposes
- `=` for a fact you establish
- `~` for the friction that fact creates
- `>>` for the ripple that fact sends through the world

Only `=` is needed to log a world. The other four core symbols record context around a fact, and you use them when that context matters to you. Everything else is optional too: modifiers, tags, headers, phases, tests. Think in concentric circles:

- **Core Notation**: facts, plus triggers, frictions, and ripples as needed
- **Modifiers and Tags** (add as needed): provisional facts, retractions, methods, tests, persistent elements
- **Structure** (for organization): world header, build session header, cycles, phases

**Start small.** Log one question with the core symbols. Add layers when they serve you.

**A note on licensing:** The CC BY-SA 4.0 license covers this specification. Worlds, logs, and settings you create with Lorelog are your own work. License them however you like.

### 1.4 Quick Start

```
? A question about your world
= The answer you decide
~ What goes wrong because of it
>> What else it changes
```

#### Quick Start Example

```
? Who controls fresh water in the city?
= The Cistern Guild owns every public well
~ The Guild raises prices in dry seasons. Riots follow.
>> [Faction:Cistern Guild|monopoly] [Tension:Water riots|seasonal]
? Who enforces the Guild's rights during a riot?
```

That last line is a new question, generated by the ripple. Writing it down right away keeps the thread of your thinking in the log.

## 2. Digital vs Analog Formats

Lorelog works in Markdown files and in paper notebooks. The notation is identical; only the wrapping differs.

### 2.1 Digital Format (Markdown)

- **World metadata** goes in YAML front matter
- **World title** is a level 1 heading
- **Build sessions** are level 2 headings (`## Build 3`)
- **Cycles** are level 3 headings (`### C7`)
- **Notation** goes in code blocks
- **Notes and prose** go between code blocks

**Always wrap notation in code blocks.** Two Lorelog symbols have a meaning in Markdown outside code blocks: a line of `=` under a line of text turns that text into a heading, and `>>` at the start of a line opens a nested blockquote. Inside a code block, both are safe.

### 2.2 Analog Format (Notebooks)

- Write headers as shown in Section 6
- Write notation directly, without code fences
- Brackets and prefixes make the page easy to scan

On paper, a small margin mark next to open `?` and `!` lines (a box you tick when answered) turns any page into a visual backlog.

### 2.3 Indentation

Indentation is optional and carries no structural meaning. Indenting the lines that belong to one cycle helps scanning:

```
? Who controls fresh water in the city?
    = The Cistern Guild owns every public well
    ~ Prices rise in dry seasons. Riots follow.
    >> [Faction:Cistern Guild|monopoly]
```

## 3. Core Notation

Five symbols follow a common loop of building a world: something demands an answer, you decide the answer, you note what it complicates, and you see what else it touches. You can use the whole loop or any part of it.

```
? or !   ->   =   ->   ~   ->   >>   ->   new ? lines
trigger      fact     friction   ripple
```

### 3.1 Triggers

A trigger records why you added something. Triggers are optional: use them when the reason is worth remembering. Lorelog has two kinds, one for each common source of demand.

**Questions (`?`)** come from curiosity. You want to understand how the world works.

```
? How does the Guild police unlicensed practitioners?
? What do farmers eat in winter?
? Why did the old empire fall?
```

**Needs (`!`)** come from a story. A scene, a chapter, an adventure, or a game session requires something the world does not yet contain. Name the story unit that creates the need:

```
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the city gate
! Adv2 needs a faction that profits from the war
! S14 needs a way to cross the marsh in one night
```

The difference is in how you judge the answer. A question is open-ended. A need has a success condition: the answer has to make the scene work. A broad need can be broken into questions:

```
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the gate
? Who guards the gates?
? What do they check?
```

You can point a trigger at the part of your setting notes it belongs to: `? Who guards the gates? (§Politics)`.

### 3.2 Facts

The `=` symbol establishes something true about the world. This is the **surface layer**: the plain answer.

```
= District wardens enforce Guild licenses, paid per arrest
= Winter staples are dried fish and barley
= The empire fell when its roads were abandoned to the forest
```

Keep one fact per line. If an answer has several parts, write several `=` lines. Separate facts can be tagged, changed, or retracted one by one.

A fact without a trigger is perfectly valid, for example when you sketch the broad strokes of a world or when an idea arrives on its own. If you are not yet sure of it, mark it provisional (`=?`, see Section 4).

### 3.3 Friction

The `~` symbol records the **tension layer**: a cost, conflict, or complication that exists because the fact is true. Who pays for it? Who resists it? What breaks under pressure?

```
= District wardens enforce Guild licenses, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests. False accusations are common.
~ Outer districts hide their healers instead of reporting them.
```

The wave shape is deliberate: friction is where the world stops being still.

Friction is optional. Some worlds and some facts are built around conflict; others are not, and a cycle without `~` is complete. When you do write friction, giving it its own symbol keeps it searchable, so you can list every tension in the world with one search.

### 3.4 Ripples

The `>>` symbol records how the fact propagates: which other elements change, which tags update, which parts of the setting are affected.

```
>> [Faction:Guild|+wardens]
>> [Tension:False arrests|open]
>> §Society: common people see the Guild as a predator, not a protector
```

New questions that the fact raises go on `?` lines right after the ripple. Their position tells you they were generated by this cycle:

```
>> [Faction:Guild|+wardens]
? Who protects people from the wardens?
? What does an arrest cost a family?
```

### 3.5 Complete Cycles

#### Curiosity cycle

```
? How does the Guild police unlicensed practitioners?
= District wardens, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests. False accusations are common.
>> [Faction:Guild|+wardens] [Tension:False arrests|open]
? Who protects people from the wardens?
```

#### Story cycle

```
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the gate
= Every traveler must show a Guild license or a warden's pass
~ Passes are sold on the black market. Forgeries are everywhere.
>> [L:North Gate|checkpoint] [Tension:Forged passes|open]
```

#### A fact on its own

```
= The city's walls are built from pale limestone
>> [L:Cistern City|+limestone walls]
```

#### A second attempt

A note can record why you revised an answer, whatever your reason was:

```
? What happens to people who cannot pay for a license?
= The Guild waives the fee for the poor
(note: I want the temples involved. Try again.)
= The Guild waives the fee for anyone a temple vouches for
~ Temples sell their vouchers. Faith becomes a fee.
```

## 4. Modifiers

The core symbols describe what the world is. Modifiers describe how firm a fact is, how you reached it, and whether it still holds. All of them are optional.

### 4.1 Provisional Facts (`=?`)

A provisional fact is a working assumption: true for now, open to change without ceremony.

```
=? The capital sits on a river delta
=? Magic is rare, maybe one person in a thousand
```

Use it for first sketches and for ideas you like but are not yet sure of. When you decide to keep a fact, confirm it by writing it again as `=`:

```
=? The capital sits on a river delta
... later ...
! Ch5 needs a flood that isolates the lower city
= The capital sits on a river delta (confirmed)
```

### 4.2 Retractions (`x=`)

Worlds change. A fact you retract is not erased; it is marked, with a reason. This is what turns the log into a history of the world's development, not just a snapshot of its current state.

```
x= Magic is free to practice
(why: Ch7 needs a black market for spells)
= Magic requires a Guild license, renewed yearly
~ Unlicensed casters trade services at night markets
>> [Rule:Magic|free→licensed] [L:Night Market|new]
```

The `x=` line quotes the old fact. The note gives the reason. The new `=` line replaces it. Six months later, you can find every retraction with one search and see why the world is the way it is.

To revise a fact partially, use the tag update syntax instead: `[L:Harbor|pop 40k→60k]`.

### 4.3 Method (`via:`)

The `via:` prefix records how an answer was reached. It is optional, like roll details in Lonelog.

```
via: decide                    (you chose it)
via: tbl Settlement Size d6=4  (a random table)
via: gen NPC Faction           (a multi-step generator)
via: ref Braudel, The Mediterranean  (a real-world source or model)
via: play Lonelog S12          (it emerged during play)
via: collab Anna               (a co-author proposed it)
```

Place `via:` between the trigger and the fact:

```
? What sits at the crossroads north of the capital?
via: tbl Landmark d8=6
= A ruined toll tower, half-swallowed by ivy
~ The Guild claims the tower. The local lord claims the road.
```

Random tables work exactly as in Lonelog: `tbl:` records any random result that gives content, from a single roll to a multi-step generator. You can define tables inline, use filtered option sets, and write multi-line results:

```
tbl: Landmark (d8)
  1-2: Shrine
  3-4: Well
  5-6: Ruined toll tower
  7: Standing stones
  8: Nothing, and that is strange

tbl: Faction
  Wants: d6=2 -> Control of trade
  Fears: d6=5 -> Exposure of its founder
  Method: d6=1 -> Debt
= [Faction:Ledger House|creditor to half the nobility]
```

### 4.4 Tests (`t:`)

The `t:` prefix records a check you run on the world and its result. The format is `t: TestName [subject] -> Result`.

```
t: Day in the life [guild practitioner, capital] -> Pass
t: Day in the life [dockworker, outer district] -> Gap: where does the food come from?
t: Scene [Ch3 gate check, 500 words] -> Pass
t: Pitch [60 seconds] -> Fail: too many factions to explain
```

A `Gap` or `Fail` result creates a trigger. Write it on the next line:

```
t: Day in the life [dockworker, outer district] -> Gap: food source
? Where does the outer district get its food?
```

Section 7.4 describes some common tests. Any check you find useful can become a test: name it, give it a subject, record the result.

### 4.5 Notes

Meta notes use parentheses, as in Lonelog. Three note types are especially useful in worldbuilding:

```
(why: the reason for a decision or retraction)
(note: a comment, doubt, or reminder)
(parked: an idea you do not want to lose, not yet part of the world)
```

`(parked: ...)` holds ideas you want to keep but have not committed to. Parking an idea keeps it out of the world without losing it.

## 5. Persistent Elements

Tags track the elements of your world across cycles and sessions: factions, places, people, rules, tensions, open questions. The grammar is the same as Lonelog's, so tags from play logs and world logs can be searched together.

### 5.1 Tag Grammar (shared with Lonelog)

```
[Type:Name|tag|tag]           first mention
[#Type:Name]                  reference to an earlier mention
[Type:Name|old→new]           explicit change
[Type:Name|+tag] [Type:Name|-tag]   add or remove a tag
[Type:Name|cat:value,value|cat:value]   categories
```

Multi-line tags work as in Lonelog:

```
[Faction:Cistern Guild
  | wants: monopoly, legitimacy
  | fears: drought ending, rival wells
  | method: pricing, wardens
]
```

### 5.2 Tag Types

- `[Faction:Name]` — Faction, institution, group. Example: `[Faction:Cistern Guild|monopoly]`
- `[N:Name]` — Named person. Example: `[N:Warden Ossa|corrupt|ambitious]`
- `[L:Name]` — Location. Example: `[L:North Gate|checkpoint]`
- `[Rule:Name]` — World rule (how something works). Example: `[Rule:Magic|licensed|costs memory]`
- `[Tension:Name]` — Persistent friction. Example: `[Tension:Water riots|seasonal]`
- `[Hist:Name]` — Historical event. Example: `[Hist:Fall of the Roads|year:-212]`
- `[Term:Name]` — In-world term or concept. Example: `[Term:vouch|temple license waiver]`
- `[Q:Name]` — Backlog question. Example: `[Q:Warden oversight|open]`
- `[Need:Name]` — Backlog story need. Example: `[Need:Ch3 gate stop|open]`

`[N:]` and `[L:]` mean the same as in Lonelog, so a location built in the world log keeps its identity when a character walks into it during play. `[Hist:]` avoids Lonelog's `[E:]`, which marks notable events *during play*; history decided at the worldbuilding table is a different thing.

Custom tag types are allowed. Declare them in the world header (Section 6) so readers know what they mean.

### 5.3 States for Tensions, Questions, and Needs

Three tag types carry a state that changes over time.

**Tensions:**

- `latent`: exists but has not erupted
- `open`: active and visible
- `seasonal` or `cyclic`: erupts on a pattern
- `resolved`: settled

**Questions and needs:**

- `open`: waiting for an answer
- `answered`: resolved, optionally with a pointer (`[Q:Warden oversight|answered→C9]`)
- `dropped`: no longer worth answering

### 5.4 Section References

When your world lives in a structured document, such as a setting bible, a wiki, or a template with named or numbered sections, point at its sections with `§`:

```
? Who guards the gates? (§Politics)
>> §Society: common people fear the wardens more than bandits
>> §2.3: add the warden ranks
```

### 5.5 One Element, Two Logs

The shared grammar is what connects building and playing. A fact that emerges during play can come back into the world, with its origin recorded:

```
(Lonelog, session 12)
? Is the North Gate guarded at night?
-> No, but...
=> Only a bribed warden, asleep. [L:North Gate|unguarded at night]

(Lorelog, build 5)
? Why is the North Gate unguarded at night?
via: play Lonelog S12
= The night shift is sold to smugglers by the warden captain
~ Honest wardens refuse the shift. They are transferred to the marshes.
>> [L:North Gate|smuggling route] [Faction:Wardens|+split]
```

## 6. Optional Structure

Structure helps you find things later. It also costs time. Use the levels that keep you oriented and skip the rest.

### 6.1 World Header

The world header is the cover page of your log. It states what you are building and how.

**Digital (YAML front matter):**

```yaml
title: The Cistern Cities
method: bottom-up, curiosity-first
author: Roberto
genre: Low fantasy, urban
pitch: A drought-stricken city where water is law and the law is for sale
world_doc: cistern-cities.md
custom_tags: [Guild:] guild ranks
start_date: 2026-09-25
last_update: 2026-09-25
```

**Analog:**

```
=== World Log: The Cistern Cities ===
[Method]           bottom-up, curiosity-first
[Genre]            Low fantasy, urban
[Pitch]            A drought-stricken city where water is law and the law is for sale
[World Doc]        Notebook 2, pages 1-12
[Start Date]       2026-09-25
```

The `method` field can name a published worldbuilding method, a generative game, `campaign prep`, a personal approach such as `top-down` or `story-first`, or anything else. The `world_doc` field points to the document, wiki, or notebook the log feeds, if one exists. Every field is optional.

### 6.2 Build Session Header

A build session is one sitting of work on the world.

**Digital:**

```markdown
## Build 3
*Date: 2026-09-27 | Duration: 1h | Phase: Structure | Cycles: C12-C18*

**Focus:** Enforcement and resistance
```

**Analog:**

```
=== Build 3 ===
[Date]      2026-09-27
[Duration]  1h
[Phase]     Structure
[Focus]     Enforcement and resistance
```

### 6.3 Cycles

A **cycle** is the unit of work: one trigger and everything that follows from it. Cycles are numbered `C1`, `C2`, `C3` and continue across the whole world, so `C47` points to one place in the log forever.

```
C7 *Guild enforcement* (§Politics)
? How does the Guild police unlicensed practitioners?
= District wardens, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests. False accusations are common.
>> [Faction:Guild|+wardens] [Tension:False arrests|open]
? Who protects people from the wardens?
```

The title in asterisks names the topic. The `§` reference names the part of the setting it touches.

**Sub-cycles (`C7.1`, `C7.2`)** handle a burst of quick answers under one topic, for example a first sketch:

```
C1 *Sketch: Power*
C1.1 ? Who rules? =? The Cistern Guild
C1.2 ? How? =? Water pricing
C1.3 ? Who resists? =? Temple of the Rain
```

**Branches (`C7a`, `C7b`)** explore alternative answers to the same trigger before you commit. Mark the one you keep:

```
C7a *Guild enforcement, option A*
= The Guild hires mercenaries
~ Mercenaries switch sides when pay is late

C7b *Guild enforcement, option B* (kept)
= District wardens, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests
```

Branches are the log's answer to a question worldbuilders ask constantly: *what if it were different?* The rejected branch stays in the log, visible and recoverable.

### 6.4 Modules

When a world grows past what one document holds comfortably, split it into modules and prefix cycles with a module code: `M1-C4`, `M2-C1`. Name modules in the world header.

```
modules: M1 The City, M2 The Marshes, M3 The Rain Faith
```

### 6.5 Phase Markers

If you work in phases (a broad sketch, then structure, then detail; continents, then regions, then cities; eras, then events), mark transitions with a line:

```
=== Phase 2: Structure ===
```

A phase change is a good place for a test (`t:`) and a note on the decision:

```
t: Pitch [60 seconds] -> Pass
(note: the sketch holds, moving to Structure)
=== Phase 2: Structure ===
```

## 7. Working Styles

The same five symbols serve very different ways of building a world. This section describes some common ones and how they look in the log. None is preferred, and most worlds mix them.

### 7.1 Story-First

When you build a world for a specific story (a novel, a comic, a campaign arc), additions tend to start from `!`. The log becomes a chain from story need to world detail.

```
C21 *Gate checks* (§Politics, §Magic)
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the gate
= Every traveler must show a Guild license or a warden's pass
~ Passes are sold on the black market
>> [L:North Gate|checkpoint] [Need:Ch3 gate stop|answered]
t: Scene [Ch3 gate check, 500 words] -> Pass
```

If you want to keep a story-first world tightly scoped, the log makes drift easy to spot:

- A `=` line with no `!` above it is detail no story asked for.
- A `!` that names no story unit may really be a `?`.
- A `?` in a story-first log shows where you started exploring.

Whether any of these is a problem is up to you.

### 7.2 Curiosity-First

When you build a world for its own sake, to explore it, to play in it later, or to find out what it contains, additions tend to start from `?`. The log becomes a chain of `Question → Answer → New Questions`.

#### The backlog

The backlog is every `[Q:]` tag with state `open`. You do not need a separate list: a search for `|open]` produces it. At the start of a build session, you can copy a snapshot:

```
(backlog snapshot, Build 4)
[Q:Warden oversight|open|p1]
[Q:Arrest cost|open|p2]
[Q:Outer district food|open|p1]
[Q:Rain Temple doctrine|open|p3]
```

The optional `p1`, `p2`, `p3` tags set priority. Pull from the top, or pull the one that interests you most today.

Pruning is a state change with a reason:

```
[Q:Rain Temple doctrine|open→dropped]
(why: open for 3 months, nothing depends on it)
```

#### Counting questions

If you want to know whether a part of the world is still opening up or settling down, count the `?` lines after each cycle's ripple:

```
t: Question count [C1-C6] -> 6 cycles, 11 new questions
```

### 7.3 Top-Down and Bottom-Up

**Top-down** building starts from the large scale (cosmology, continents, empires, eras) and works down to towns and people. **Bottom-up** building starts from one village, one character, or one street and works outward only as far as it needs to.

Both work in Lorelog. They leave different shapes in the log:

- A top-down log often opens with sub-cycles of `=?` facts sketching the big picture, then confirms or retracts them as detail arrives.
- A bottom-up log often opens with a single `?` about something small and close, and its ripples reach outward: the `>>` lines name larger and larger elements over time.

```
(top-down)
C1 *Sketch: The continent*
C1.1 ? How many great powers? =? Three
C1.2 ? What divides them? =? A dying inland sea

(bottom-up)
C1 *The canal market*
? What does a fishmonger at the canal market fear?
= The Guild's water tax on the eel tanks
~ She cannot pay it in the dry season
>> [Faction:Cistern Guild|taxes water] [Tension:Water tax|seasonal]
? Why is water scarce enough to tax?
```

### 7.4 Common Tests

Tests are optional. They help when you want to know whether a part of the world is ready, whether for a story, a session, or simply for you. A few that work across styles:

| Test | Question it asks | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day in the life | Can an ordinary inhabitant get through a day? | `t: Day in the life [dockworker]` |
| Scene | Can I write or run this scene with what exists? | `t: Scene [Ch3 gate check]` |
| Pitch | Can I explain the world in a minute? | `t: Pitch [60 seconds]` |
| Hooks | How many conflicts could start a story? | `t: Hooks [the city] -> 3` |
| Question count | How many new questions did these cycles raise? | `t: Question count [C1-C6]` |
| Consistency | Does this fact contradict anything? | `t: Consistency [C12] -> Pass` |

A multi-line `t:` block records a test with several parts. Use `=` where the world already answers and `Gap` where it does not:

```
t: Day in the life [dockworker, outer district]
  Wakes: = tenement by the dry canal
  Eats: Gap
  Friction before noon: = the water queue at the Guild well
  Depends on: = the foreman who holds his work token
  Fears: = a warden raid on the tenement
-> Gap
? Where does the outer district get its food?
[Q:Outer district food|open|p1]
```

Every `Gap` becomes a new backlog entry. When all lines hold `=`, the result is `Pass`.

### 7.5 Switching Styles

Many worlds start curiosity-first and become story-first when a story appears. Others start top-down and turn bottom-up once the map is drawn. The log can hold all of it. Record the switch in the header (`method:` with the new value and the date) or with a note. The two trigger types keep the main distinction clear: `?` lines are curiosity, `!` lines are story.

## 8. Other Contexts

The loop of trigger, fact, friction, and ripple is not specific to one activity. Any work where a world is decided step by step can use Lorelog.

### 8.1 Generative Worldbuilding Games

Many games build a world through prompts, cards, or turns, as in *Microscope* or *The Quiet Year*. The prompt is the trigger. The answer is the fact. The game's own consequences become frictions and ripples.

```
C4 *Spring, week 3*
? (prompt) A stranger arrives with news of a far-off danger. What is it?
via: game card draw
= Traders report that the upriver dam is cracking
~ The Guild denies it. Denial protects its prices.
>> [Tension:Dam collapse|latent] [Clock:Dam 1/6]
```

Progress elements from Lonelog (`[Clock:]`, `[Track:]`, `[Timer:]`) work here unchanged, for games that track pressure over turns.

### 8.2 Collaborative Worldbuilding

When several people build together, attribute any line with a parenthetical name, in the same way Lonelog attributes actions:

```
?(Anna) Is there a religion that opposes the Guild?
=(Roberto) The Temple of the Rain teaches that water is a gift, not a good
~(Anna) The Temple buys its own wells in secret
>> [Faction:Temple of the Rain|+secret wells]
```

The log then shows who proposed each piece, which helps when a collaborator asks why something is the way it is.

### 8.3 Campaign Prep

A game master preparing a setting works story-first: the "story" is the next session. Use `!` with the session as the story unit:

```
! Session 4 needs a reason for the party to enter the marshes
= The only doctor who can treat the fever lives in the marsh village
~ The marsh village refuses entry to anyone with a Guild license
>> [L:Marsh Village|closed to Guild] [N:Doctor Helve|exiled]
```

After the session, facts that emerged at the table come back into the log with `via: play` (see §5.5).

### 8.4 History and Timelines

Historical events use `[Hist:]` tags with a date category. A search for `[Hist:` then gives an unordered list, and a sort by the `year` value gives a timeline:

```
? Why are the roads north abandoned?
= The empire stopped maintaining them after a plague
~ The Guild owes its power to the collapse of overland trade
>> [Hist:Fall of the Roads|year:-212] [Faction:Cistern Guild|origin:road collapse]
```

### 8.5 Languages, Names, and Terms

The same loop works for naming systems and constructed languages: a question about the language, a decision, the irregularity or conflict it creates, and its ripple through the lexicon.

```
? How does the city's language form the word for a guild member?
= Suffix -ren on the craft noun: cistern-ren, ward-ren
~ Old families use the archaic -reth, and resent the new form
>> [Term:-ren|agent suffix] [Term:-reth|archaic, prestige]
```

### 8.6 Mapping a Published Method

If you follow a published worldbuilding method, map its concepts onto Lorelog once and write the mapping in your world header or in a note. Most methods have some version of the same parts: where ideas come from, how they are checked, and how changes are recorded.

As an example, this is the mapping for the Lean Worldbuilding Method (LWM), which has a Story Mode and an Exploration Mode:

| LWM concept | Lorelog notation |
| --- | --- |
| Story need (Story Mode) | `!` trigger, `[Need:]` tag |
| Queued question (Exploration Mode) | `?` trigger, `[Q:]` tag |
| Two-Layer Rule: surface and tension | `=` fact and `~` friction |
| Evolution Log | The log itself, plus `x=` and `(why: ...)` |
| Question Log | The chain of cycles: `?` then `=` then new `?` |
| Seed keywords | `=?` provisional facts in sub-cycles |
| Inhabitant Test, scene test, pitch | `t:` lines |
| Decision gates | Phase markers with `t:` lines |

The same exercise works for any method: list its concepts, find the Lorelog symbol or tag that carries each one, and declare anything left over as a custom convention (§8.7).

### 8.7 Writing Your Own Conventions

If your practice needs a new prefix, tag type, or test, declare it in the world header and use it consistently. Keep the five core symbols intact. The core is what makes one person's log readable to another.

## 10. Best Practices

These practices keep a log easy to read and search. None of them says how to build a world.

**Do: keep one fact per line.** Single facts can be tagged, confirmed, or retracted one at a time.

**Do: retract, don't erase.** `x=` plus `(why: ...)` keeps the history of the world readable.

**Do: write a trigger when the reason matters.** Six months later, the `?` or `!` above a fact is often the only record of why it exists.

**Do: write new questions down when they occur to you.** A `?` line after a ripple costs a few seconds and keeps the thought.

**Don't: copy your setting notes into the log.** The notes hold the current state; the log holds the decisions. Link them with `§` references.

**Don't: skip the code blocks in Markdown.** Outside them, `=` and `>>` change the formatting of your file.

### A.1 Core Symbols

| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `?` | Question | A trigger from curiosity |
| `!` | Need | A trigger from a story unit |
| `=` | Fact | The surface answer |
| `~` | Friction | The tension the fact creates |
| `>>` | Ripple | The effect on other elements |

## Appendix B. FAQ

**Q: Do I need a particular worldbuilding method to use Lorelog?** A: No. Lorelog records decisions, whatever produced them. If you follow a method, map its concepts once (§8.6) and log as usual.

**Q: Do I need a world document to use Lorelog?** A: No. The log alone can hold a world. A document is useful when the world grows and you want its current state in one place.

**Q: Can I use Lorelog and Lonelog in the same file?** A: Yes, but keep them in separate sections. The tags are shared on purpose; the core symbols are not. A `=` in Lorelog is a fact, while `=>` in Lonelog is a consequence of play.

**Q: What if an answer has no friction?** A: Then write no `~` line. Friction is there for when you want to record it.

**Q: Should every idea go into the log?** A: That is up to you. A common split is: ideas you commit to go in as `=` or `=?`, and ideas you want to keep for later go into `(parked: ...)` notes.

**Q: How do I share a world log with readers who do not know Lorelog?** A: Share the Quick Start (§1.4). The five symbols are enough to read most logs.

## Appendix C. Symbol Design

- **`?`** is the universal sign of a question, and matches Lonelog.
- **`!`** signals demand: a story needs something, now.
- **`=`** states that something is so. It reads as "is" or "equals".
- **`~`** is a wave, the shape of instability. Friction is where the world stops being still.
- **`>>`** shows propagation: the fact flows outward into the rest of the world.

In digital files, always use code blocks: `=` under a text line and `>>` at the start of a line have Markdown meanings outside them.
